# Scrum Ceremonies Sanity

Every ceremony must earn its calendar slot. If nobody could say what it produced, cancel it.

## Per Ceremony: Purpose, Output, Smell
- **Planning:** Purpose: commit to a goal. Output: a sprint goal and a realistic plan. Smell: reading tickets aloud for two hours.
- **Daily standup:** Purpose: coordinate and surface blockers. Output: unblocked people. Smell: status reports to a manager.
- **Refinement:** Purpose: make upcoming work ready (see `story-slicing`, `requirements-grilling`). Smell: first time anyone sees the story is in planning.
- **Review/demo:** Purpose: get feedback on working software. Smell: slides instead of software.
- **Retro:** Purpose: improve how we work. Output: one or two owned experiments. Smell: the same complaints every sprint.

## Health Checks
- Is there a sprint **goal**, not just a ticket pile?
- Does the team finish what it starts? Chronic carryover means over-commitment.
- Are velocity numbers used to compare teams or pressure people? Stop. Velocity is a planning aid for one team.
- Do experiments from retros actually get done?

## Guardrails
- Process serves delivery, not the reverse. Adapt it, and write down why.
- Do not add a ceremony to fix a problem a conversation would solve.

## Output Format
A short **diagnosis** per ceremony (keep, shorten, change, or drop), plus **one or two experiments** to try next sprint, each with a way to tell whether it worked.
